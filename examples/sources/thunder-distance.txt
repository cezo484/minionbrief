Thunder is the sound made when lightning rapidly heats the air around the
bolt. The heated air expands faster than the speed of sound, creating a
shock wave that we hear as a clap or a rumble. A nearby strike sounds like
a sharp crack; a distant strike sounds like a long, low rumble because the
sound waves scatter and the higher frequencies fade first.

You can estimate how far away a strike was by counting the seconds between
the flash and the thunder. Sound covers roughly one kilometre in three
seconds, so dividing the count by three gives the distance in kilometres.
For example, a nine second gap means the strike was about three kilometres
away.
