Lightning is a large electrical discharge between a thundercloud and the
ground, or between two parts of a cloud. The discharge heats the air around
it to about 30,000 kelvin in a fraction of a second, which is roughly five
times hotter than the surface of the Sun. The flash we see is the light
from this superheated air channel.

We see the flash before we hear the thunder because light travels much
faster than sound. Light moves at about 300,000 kilometres per second,
while sound in air moves at about 343 metres per second at 20 degrees
Celsius. The light from a strike a few kilometres away reaches our eyes
almost instantly, while the sound takes several seconds to arrive.
